The story of our neighbour

Hawa Mahal, the Palace of Winds

In 1799, Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh of Jaipur built Hawa Mahal as an extension of the City Palace. Its architect, Lal Chand Ustad, shaped the façade like the crown of Lord Krishna, whom the Maharaja deeply revered.

The palace was made for the royal women, who followed purdah. From behind its 953 small jharokha windows, they could watch festivals and processions on the street below without being seen. The latticed windows also let the breeze flow through every room, keeping the palace cool in Jaipur's summers. That breeze gave it its name: Hawa Mahal, the Palace of Winds.
